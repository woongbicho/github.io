About

About Dr. Woongbi Cho (조웅비, 曺雄飛)

I am a polymer materials scientist and postdoctoral scholar in Mechanical Engineering at Stanford University. My research sits at the boundary of soft robotics, polymer chemistry, sustainable materials, and device engineering.

During my Ph.D. training, I developed shape-reconfigurable liquid crystalline polymer systems, sulfur-rich optical and energy materials, and bio-derived composite processing strategies. These projects share a central idea: materials become more powerful when their chemistry, architecture, and processing history are designed together.

At Stanford, I build on this foundation to study electromagnetic-driven soft robots, active metamaterials, and polymer assembly mechanisms that translate external fields into useful motion and adaptive function.

Research Topics, Materials, and Tools

A unified materials platform spanning programmable shape, waste valorization, and sustainable composite processing.

01

Shape-Reconfigurable Devices and Active Metamaterials

Liquid crystalline polymers, covalent adaptable networks, stiffness gradients, and magnetic/light-responsive composites that encode motion, snap-through, locomotion, and adaptive surface function directly into material architecture.

Representative papers Science Advances 2025 Advanced Functional Materials 2021 Nano Energy 2023 Advanced Materials Technologies 2020
02

Waste-Sulfur Valorization for Optics and Energy

Sulfur-rich polymers that transform petroleum-refining byproducts into high-index infrared optics, MWIR polarizers, 4D-printable networks, and PFAS-free triboelectric generators with MXene-segregated charge pathways.

Representative papers Advanced Materials 2024 Accounts of Materials Research 2024 Advanced Materials 2023 Advanced Optical Materials 2023
03

Bio-Derived Polymer Composite Processing

Cellulose-, lignin-, and biomass-derived composite routes that connect functional group chemistry, compounding, foam/film processing, and structure-property-performance relationships for sustainable manufacturing.

Representative papers Cellulose 2023 Chemical Engineering Journal 2024

Materials

Liquid crystalline polymer networks, sulfur-rich polymers, covalent adaptable networks, MXene composites, reduced graphene oxide patterns, cellulose derivatives, lignin polyurethane foams, and thermoplastic polyurethane composites.

Tools

Inverse vulcanization, vacuum-assisted thermal treatment, microtexturing, replica molding, 4D printing, IR optical characterization, triboelectric device testing, electromagnetic and magnetic actuation, and processing-structure-property mapping.
